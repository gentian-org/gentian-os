# Gentian OS v0.4

**Milestone:** the customization ladder, tenant backup and restore, and an
installer that converges.
**Compares against:** [v0.3.0](https://github.com/gentian-org/gentian-os/releases/tag/v0.3.0)
(2026-07-18) — 795 commits.
**Cut with:** the runbook in [deployment.md §5.4](../deployment.md).

v0.3 finished the app story: apps installed, authenticated and, with the AI
line, talked to a model. v0.4 is about what happens to an app *after* it is
installed — how a customer changes it without forking it, how their data gets
out and back in, and how the cluster that runs it is built from a description
rather than from an operator's shell history.

Three subsystems carry that, and each replaces something that used to be
implicit: the **customization ladder** replaces "patch it wherever you know
how", **tenant export/restore** replaces "we have volume snapshots somewhere",
and the **step driver** replaces `install.sh` + `update.sh` + `uninstall.sh` and
the knowledge that lived only in whoever ran them.

---

## 1. The customization ladder

The headline, designed and delivered in this release:
[app-customization.md](../app-customization.md).

Gentian had customization *mechanisms* — `extraValues`, tenant config, sidecar
addons, an Odoo fork — and no ordering over them. Nothing told a human or an
agent which mechanism was the cheapest one that could express a change, which
ones a given app supported, or what the change would cost at the next upstream
release. v0.4 supplies that ordering.

- **Seven rungs, L0–L6** (configure, drop-in, companion, extension, repackage,
  patch, fork) ordered by how much of the app's own delivery artifact Gentian
  ends up owning — which is what determines upgrade cost. Cluster hotfixes are
  Rung X and forbidden.
- **A second axis, scope** — tenant / profile / platform blast radius, chosen
  independently of the rung.
- **`AppProfile.spec.customization`** — each app declares which rungs are
  reachable and by which mechanism, plus an A–D readiness grade. Generic advice
  is useless; "Odoo supports L3 via addons, Collabora does not" is actionable.
- **The `Customization` CRD** — a DEP-3-inspired record required for every
  customization at L2 and above, carrying owner, origin, exit criteria and a
  review date, so a downstream delta is a debt with a due date rather than a
  decision. A CI validator and a debt report enforce it; the report surfaces in
  the Admin Console.
- **The addon model (L3)** — addons are `AppProfile`s that activate inside a
  base rather than deploying alone. They need no chart of their own, inherit
  the base's ladder, and are selected per tenant through
  `Tenant.spec.apps[].addons` and a PUT endpoint. Commercial addons are
  withheld until an entitlement grants them.
- **Editions `ce · me · ee`** — how an entry is licensed, not who publishes it.
  `spec.author` names the supplier, which may be the upstream organisation or a
  third party. `AppPackage` presets pre-select a curated addon set at install
  without becoming an artifact of their own.
- **Tenant-scoped L1 drop-ins** — a tenant admin can add a config or asset file
  through the console, rendered by the operator into a ConfigMap.

The framework is authorship-neutral by design: `Customization.spec.origin`
records whether a customization came from Gentian, a tenant, a supplier or a
partner from day one. v0.4 ships that data model; the delegation *process*
(signing, entitlement, review SLAs) is deliberately deferred.

## 2. Tenant backup, export and restore

A whole subsystem that did not exist in v0.3, documented for the people who use
it in [tenant-backup-guide.md](../tenant-backup-guide.md).

A **`TenantExport`** captures a workspace — every app's database, its object
storage, its PersistentVolumeClaims, its staged Secrets and its member accounts
— into one encrypted bundle. **`TenantRestore`** puts it back. Around those:

- **`BackupPolicy` / `TenantBackupPolicy`** set destination and schedule as
  policy: cluster first, then tenant. A bundle can be sent to a destination the
  policy does not name, reusing the workspace credential or taking one-off keys.
- **`TenantExportSchedule`** takes exports on a cadence, anchored on the window
  it committed to rather than drifting with each firing.
- **Encryption** by recipient key or passphrase, so the platform can hold a
  bundle it cannot read.
- **Quiescing** per `AppProfile.spec.backup` — scale to zero, or run the app's
  own pre/post hooks in its own pod under its own role. Consistency is
  per-app by default, `perStore` where the app says so.
- **`minRestoreVersion`** refuses to restore a bundle into an app older than
  the version that wrote it; the reverse direction is allowed, because that
  just runs the app's migrations.

Schedule, retention, encryption and destination are deliberately absent from
the profile: they are platform and tenant policy, and an app author must not be
able to weaken them.

Cluster-level PITR and Velero are **not** in v0.4. What ships is tenant-scoped
export and restore, plus the credential escrow and the recovery drills.

## 3. Identity provisioning moved onto Crossplane

In v0.3 a tenant's Keycloak configuration was written by a series of bootstrap
Jobs. Those Jobs and the Compositions were two writers over the same objects,
which flapped, and no single artifact described a tenant realm.

v0.4 moves the whole surface into `tenant-default` one object at a time, in a
repeated observe → declare → manage → *retire the Job* sequence: the tenant
realm and its user profile, the browser flow and login theme, the
first-broker-login flow and its executions, the reverse broker into the kernel
realm, the OIDC pack's client roles and protocol mappers, the entitlement
groups and their grants, and the required actions.

The Jobs are gone. Their **names** survive, in a single documented sweep
(`keycloak_retired_jobs.go`), purely to delete what they left behind on clusters
that ran them — a retired Job is not harmless where it sits, because its TTL
expires and an older image or a stale Argo revision recreates it against objects
that now have a declared owner.

## 4. The installer became a driver

`install.sh` no longer installs anything. It is a driver over
`scripts/steps/*.sh`, each of which declares `requires:`, `provides:` and
`mutates:` and implements `check()`, `apply()` and `destroy()`. Ordering is the
filename prefix, so the sequence is visible from `ls`. See
[install-reference.md](../install-reference.md).

- **`uninstall.sh` and `update.sh` are deleted.** Reversal is `destroy()` per
  step (`--purge` also removes the data an uninstall keeps); update is a
  convergent re-run, because `check()` makes every step idempotent.
- **One command, then one handover.** The install writes the cluster's recovery
  kit itself, at the one moment when the derivation salt, the master password
  and the OpenBao recovery key all exist and none is durable yet, and records
  the handover in a ConfigMap. Leaving that to a command the operator had to
  know about meant it was routinely missed.
- **Scoped runs** — `--phase`, `--from`, `--until`, `--skip`, `--only`,
  `--dry-run`, `--explain`, `--status`, `--verify-only`.
- **`credentials.yaml`** is the credential catalogue: every secret that must
  come from outside the cluster, in one file, with the `CredentialRequirement`
  CRs generated from it and `make verify-gen` asserting the two carriers agree.
  Derived credentials (HMAC of the master password) and generated ones are
  absent by design — they need no human. Prompting is catalogue-driven, so a
  cluster is only asked for what it actually uses, and Compositions gate on
  credential satisfaction rather than on shell ordering.
- **A Credential Manager service** with ESO satisfaction probes and per-field
  validation, so a wrong value is attributed to the field that carries it.
- **`versions.yaml`** pins every external component the installer pulls —
  Crossplane, cert-manager, ESO — once, as platform source rather than
  per-cluster configuration. Two clusters on the same release run the same
  versions.
- **`cluster-settings.env` is retired.** Its declarative half is on the Cluster
  claim; claims arrive through Argo CD.

## 5. Mail became a real service

`MAIL_SERVICE_MODE=kernel` now actually delivers, in both directions.

Postfix and Dovecot run in the kernel with per-tenant DKIM signing over
published OpenDKIM tables, port 25 exposed so the cluster can receive internet
mail, IMAP over TLS, and a mail password minted per user per client app instead
of a cluster-wide master user. The outbound address is a cluster declaration
rather than a hardcoded relay, Postfix is pinned to the node that sends, and
**external-dns** publishes the tenant mail records — and the rest of the
cluster's records — from the cluster itself.

## 6. Edge, gateway and infrastructure

- **One Gateway serves every hostname**, with the portal reachable on tenant
  hostnames and `:80` redirecting to `:443`. Load-balancer addressing is
  provider-agnostic, with an OpenStack health monitor where the provider offers
  one.
- **Kernel service manifests are templated on stage** instead of copied per
  environment; cert-manager manifests render through Helm; a declarable trust
  anchor supports an offline self-signed CA.
- **Dynamic database creation on PostgreSQL**, generic privileged-role
  provisioning (`PrivilegedRoleSpec`) in place of app-specific code in the
  kernel, and Redis ACL usernames mapped through `valueMapping`. Apps declare
  their quirks; the operator no longer names apps.
- **Argo CD 3.5.0**, with the operator's RBAC completed and rollback no longer
  hanging.
- **`ResourcePlan`** — a tenant's CPU, memory and storage ceiling is a
  cluster object resolved from a plan catalogue, not a hand-set quota. Design:
  [design/resource-plans.md](../design/resource-plans.md).
- **Node pool migration** is documented as a maintenance procedure:
  [node-pool-migration.md](../node-pool-migration.md).

## 7. Portability and supply chain

The installer runs on stock **bash 3.2**, so macOS works: BSD/GNU compat
primitives, a portability lint, and a macOS CI job that runs the driver on the
runner's own bash. The operator image is **multi-arch** (`linux/amd64`,
`linux/arm64`) with manifest-list digest pins and a lint that keeps them honest.

CI also gained step-contract linting, a recovery-kit round trip, credential
catalogue parity, and authorization policy tests.

## 8. New APIs

| CRD | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Customization` | The record required for every customization at rung ≥ L2 |
| `AppPackage` | A curated addon preset, pre-selected at install |
| `TenantExport` | One capture of a tenant into an encrypted bundle |
| `TenantRestore` | Putting a bundle back |
| `TenantExportSchedule` | Exports on a cadence |
| `BackupPolicy` | Cluster-level destination and retention |
| `TenantBackupPolicy` | The tenant's own override within the cluster's |
| `CredentialRequirement` | One entry of the credential catalogue |
| `ResourcePlan` | A tenant's resource ceiling, as a plan |

`AppProfile` gained `spec.customization`, `spec.backup`, `spec.author`,
`spec.edition` and `spec.provisioning.privilegedRole`.

## 9. Upgrade notes

- **`uninstall.sh` and `update.sh` no longer exist.** Use `./install.sh` for
  both directions: a convergent re-run to update, `--purge` to tear down.
- **`cluster-settings.env` is retired** — its settings live on the Cluster
  claim. `install.sh` reads `install.env` and the claim.
- **`GENTIAN_OS_BRANCH` must be set to pin a cluster to this release.**
  Checking out the tag is not enough; a detached checkout falls back to
  `develop`. See [deployment.md §4](../deployment.md).
- **The edition enum is `ce`/`me`/`ee`.** `pro` is no longer accepted; one
  catalogue profile carried it and was migrated. `ee` now means *commercially
  licensed and entitlement-gated*, with `spec.author` naming the supplier.
- **`spec.adminEmail` is gone** — a tenant admin's login is their address.
- Removed because nothing read or enforced them: the init-Job sizing fields,
  the mail quota and rate limit, and the TURN plumbing.

## 10. Deliberately not in v0.4

- Cluster-level PITR and Velero (backup plan Phase 6).
- Automated customization-readiness grading — grades are maintainer-assigned;
  the rubric automation is roadmap item 2.13.
- Third-party customization delegation: signing, entitlement and review SLAs.
  The data model carries authorship; the process does not exist yet.
- Migrating tenant provisioning *entirely* to Crossplane. The identity surface
  moved; whether the rest should follow is roadmap item 2.1.
